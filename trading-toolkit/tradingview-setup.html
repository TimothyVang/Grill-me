Bitcoin is a different beast Bitcoin moves thousands of dollars a day — far more than oil or the S&P. That means your stops have to be much wider in dollar terms, so you must trade fewer contracts to keep the same risk. Even one full BTC1! contract can risk thousands on a normal stop — which is exactly why you start on MBT1!. Same playbook, same levels; just respect the size. (For a truly 24/7 reference chart, BTCUSD shows spot crypto, but trade the futures ticker on TopStep.)
SPY vs ES — don't confuse them SPY is the S&P ETF (a stock you buy shares of); ES/MES are the S&P futures you'll trade on TopStep. They move almost identically, so you can study either — but trade the futures ticker, since that's your actual instrument. Type SPY only if you want the ETF view for reference.

02  /  The one honest catch

Free vs. real-time data

TradingView's free plan is perfect for learning — charting, drawing, indicators, and the paper-trading account are all free. The catch: live CME futures data (CL, ES) is usually delayed ~10 minutes on the free tier.

  • For learning the playbook — drawing Fib, marking levels, reading candles on the daily and 5-min — delayed data is totally fine. Do this for weeks first.
  • For live paper-trading practice with real timing — you'll eventually want the CME real-time data add-on (a few dollars a month). Don't pay for it until you're ready to practice execution.
Order of spending Free charts → free paper account → (only when your journal shows an edge) → real-time data → real broker → real money on micros. Most people skip to the last step and lose. You won't.

03  /  Build the chart

Set it up once

  1. Make a free account & open the chart

    Go to tradingview.com, sign up, click Chart. In the top-left symbol box, type MCL1! and hit enter. You're looking at micro crude oil.

  2. Set the chart type to candles

    Top toolbar → chart-type dropdown → Candles (or Hollow Candles). This is how you read the open/high/low/close from the playbook.

  3. Set your two timeframes

    Top-left interval box. Use Daily (D) to map your big levels, and 5-minute (5) to trade. Save both by clicking the star ⭐ next to the interval so they're one click away.

  5. Draw your levels & Fibonacci

    Left-side drawing toolbar. Use the Horizontal Line / Ray for support, resistance, prior-day high/low, and the all-time high. Use the Fib Retracement tool to drag swing-low → swing-high (see the playbook for exactly how).

  6. Save it as a template & build a watchlist

    Save the layout so every symbol inherits your indicators. Add MCL1!, CL1!, MES1!, ES1!, MBT1!, and BTC1! to a watchlist (right-side panel) so you can flip between them instantly.

04  /  When to watch

Best hours for each market

Both trade nearly 24 hours, but you want the windows with real volume — that's when the levels behave and moves are clean. Times are US Eastern.

Oil · CL / MCL
9:00 – 11:30 AM ET

The US morning session. Plus Wednesday 10:30 AM ET — the weekly EIA inventory report, which moves oil hard. As a beginner, avoid trading the first minute of that report; watch how it reacts.

S&P · ES / MES
9:30 – 11:30 AM ET

The US cash-market open — the highest-volume, cleanest window. Also active around 8:30 AM ET economic data. The lunch hours (12–2 PM) go quiet and choppy; newer traders lose money there.

Bitcoin · BTC / MBT
Nearly 24 hours

CME crypto futures trade almost round-the-clock, Sunday evening to Friday, with a short daily break — and spot crypto never closes. Cleanest volume still overlaps the US session. Beware weekends: futures close but spot keeps moving, so Monday can open with a gap.

Beginner rule Pick one market and one 2-hour window. Don't watch four charts at once on day one — you'll overtrade. Master oil's morning or the S&P open first, then expand.

05  /  Every session

Your daily routine

Same steps, every day, before you take a single trade. This is the boring habit that actually builds an edge.

  • Mark levels on the DailyPrior day high/low, key support/resistance, all-time high. Do this before the session opens.
  • Note the trendOn the 5-min: is the 9-EMA above or below the 21? Above VWAP or below? That's your bias for the day.
  • Wait for confluencePrice at a level + Fib golden pocket + a rejection candle. No confluence = no trade. Sit on your hands.
  • Size it before you clickRun the playbook's calculator. Risk ≤ 1%. Know your stop and target before entering.
  • Journal every tradeScreenshot the setup, note why you entered and the result. After 30–50 trades your journal tells you if you actually have an edge.
  • Stop at your daily limitDown two trades? Walk away. Protecting the account beats revenge-trading every single time.
