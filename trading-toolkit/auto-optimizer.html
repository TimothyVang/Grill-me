Why the test column is usually a bloodbath The optimizer guarantees it will find a rule set that looks great in-sample — with hundreds of tries, some win by pure luck. That's overfitting, and it's not learning, it's memorizing noise. The only number that matters is out-of-sample expectancy. If the best training strategy is positive in-sample but ≤0 out-of-sample, it learned nothing useful. A strategy that stays positive on unseen data is worth a closer look — but even then it's a candidate for paper trading, never a guarantee.
This is the honest ceiling of "self-learning" This is real machine learning: search a space, score against data, validate out-of-sample. It is not a system that predicts the future or trades your money by itself. No amount of optimization changes that. Use it to reject bad ideas cheaply and to stay humble about the good ones.

Education, not financial advice. An optimizer fits rules to historical data; past performance never guarantees future results, and out-of-sample success on samples does not imply live profitability. Companion to the Strategy Lab.
