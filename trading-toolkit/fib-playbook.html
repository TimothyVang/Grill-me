The Confluence Playbook

How to read candles, map support and all-time highs, draw Fibonacci, stack indicators — and size every trade so one bad call can't end your account. Built for TopStep evaluations on oil and the S&P.

00  /  Start here

Nobody predicts the market. You manage risk.

Every tool in this guide — Fibonacci, support and resistance, all-time-high maps, moving averages — does one honest thing: it marks zones where the odds tilt slightly in your favor. None of them tells the future. If they did, the person who found the formula wouldn't be selling it.

So the game isn't "be right." Good traders are wrong 40–50% of the time and still make money, because when they're right they make more than they lose when they're wrong. That only works if every trade has a pre-planned exit and a fixed dollar risk. That single habit — not any indicator — is what gets people through a TopStep evaluation.

The one rule that matters most Risk a small, fixed slice of your account per trade — 0.5% to 1%. On a $50,000 TopStep account with a daily loss limit near $1,000, that's roughly $250–$500 of risk per trade, max. Decide your exit before you enter. The calculator further down does this math for you.

What a chart can do

Show where price reacted before, where buyers/sellers showed up, and where risk is well-defined. It gives you repeatable setups with a clear "I was wrong" price.

What a chart can't do

Tell you what happens next. News, a headline, or a big fund can invalidate any pattern instantly. That's why the stop exists — and why you never remove it.

01  /  Reading price

Reading a candle

A candlestick packs four numbers into one shape, over a fixed slice of time (a "5-minute candle" covers five minutes). The body is the open-to-close range; the wicks are how far price stretched and got rejected.

The handful of signals worth knowing

  • Long lower wick (hammer) at a support zone — buyers defended that price. Interest for longs.
  • Long upper wick (shooting star) at resistance or an all-time high — sellers rejected higher prices. Interest for shorts.
  • Engulfing candle — a big candle that fully covers the prior one, in the opposite direction. A shift in who's in control.
  • Big body, small wicks — strong momentum. Small body, big wicks (doji) — indecision; the market is undecided.
Context is everything A hammer in the middle of nowhere means little. The same hammer landing exactly on a support level that also lines up with a Fibonacci retracement — that's a signal. One candle alone is noise; a candle at a level is information. That layering is what "confluence" means, and it's the whole idea behind this playbook.

02  /  Mapping the field

Support, resistance & the all-time high

Before any indicator, mark the horizontal levels. These are prices where the market has turned before — and markets have memory, because traders place orders where they got hurt or made money last time.

  • Support — a price floor buyers previously defended. Price tends to bounce there… until it doesn't (a "break").
  • Resistance — a price ceiling sellers previously defended.
  • All-time high (ATH) — the highest price ever traded. There's no overhead resistance above it, so breakouts can run — but false breakouts above an ATH are also common traps. Treat a fresh ATH as either a breakout or a rejection, and let the candle tell you which.
  • Flip / retest — once broken, old resistance often becomes new support (and vice versa). The retest of a broken level is one of the cleanest, lowest-risk entries there is.

How to draw them (do this once per session)

  1. Zoom out to the daily chart. Mark the 3–5 most obvious highs and lows price keeps reacting to. Fewer, stronger lines beat a cluttered chart.
  2. Mark the current all-time high and the prior day's high and low (day traders lean on these constantly).
  3. Drop to your trading timeframe (e.g. 5-min for CL, 5- or 15-min for ES) and note where those daily levels fall on your screen. Those are your battle lines for the day.

03  /  The golden ratio

Fibonacci retracements & extensions

After a strong move, price rarely goes straight — it pulls back before continuing. Fibonacci retracement levels predict how far a pullback is likely to reach. They come from the golden ratio (0.618) that shows up throughout the Fibonacci sequence, and traders watch them so widely that they become partly self-fulfilling.

LevelWhat it meansHow traders use it
0.236Shallow pullbackVery strong trend; price barely dips
0.382Modest pullbackCommon in healthy trends
0.500Halfway backNot a true Fib, but widely watched
0.618The "golden pocket" (with 0.65)The most-watched entry zone for continuation
0.786Deep pullbackLast stand before the move is likely dead

How to draw it, exactly

  • Uptrend: use the Fib tool, click the recent swing low, drag to the swing high. The levels appear as pullback targets below.
  • Downtrend: click the swing high, drag to the swing low. Levels become bounce targets above.
  • The trade: wait for price to reach the 0.618 pocket and print a reversal candle (hammer/engulfing) there. Enter, stop just beyond 0.786, target the prior high (or a Fib extension like 1.272 / 1.618 for where the move might reach next).
Why it's not magic Fibonacci "works" partly because so many traders draw the same lines and place orders there. It fails all the time on its own. It becomes powerful only when the golden pocket lands on top of a support level you already marked, or a moving average — confluence again.

04  /  The instruments

Three indicators, and what they actually tell you

More indicators is not more edge — it's more contradiction. Pick a few that answer different questions and learn them cold. Here's a lean, honest set for futures.

VWAP · fair value

Volume-Weighted Average Price — the average price weighted by volume, reset each day. It's the intraday reference for ES/SPY: institutions benchmark to it. Price above VWAP = buyers in control; below = sellers. Pullbacks to VWAP are common entry spots.

EMA (9 & 21) · trend

Exponential moving averages track trend direction and act as dynamic support/resistance. When the 9 is above the 21 and both point up, you favor longs. When price pulls back to the EMAs and bounces — that's your trend-continuation entry.

RSI (14) · momentum

Measures momentum 0–100. Above 70 "overbought," below 30 "oversold" — but in a strong trend it can stay pinned for a long time, so never short just because RSI is high. Its real value is divergence: price makes a new high but RSI doesn't → momentum is fading.

About "LMS" and prediction LMS (least-mean-squares) is a real signal-processing filter, and adaptive filters do get used in quantitative finance — but by teams with tick-level data, execution infrastructure, and PhDs, and even they don't "win every trade." It is not something you bolt onto a TopStep chart to forecast candles. Don't let anyone sell you an "LMS predictor." The indicators above, read as context rather than crystal balls, will serve you far better.

05  /  Putting it together

The confluence checklist

Here's the whole method in one place. Do not take a trade unless several of these line up at the same price. The more boxes checked, the better the setup — and if only one is checked, you pass. There's always another trade.

  • Trend agreesHigher timeframe direction supports your trade. 9/21 EMA stacked in your direction.
  • You're at a levelA marked support/resistance, VWAP, prior day high/low, or all-time high.
  • Fibonacci confirmsThe 0.618 golden pocket lands on that same level. Confluence.
  • The candle confirmsA hammer, engulfing, or clear rejection candle prints at the level. Wait for it to close.
  • Risk is defined & smallStop has an obvious home (beyond the level); dollar risk ≤ 1% of the account. Reward ≥ 2× the risk.

Two concrete examples

Oil — LONG CL/MCL

CL rallies hard off the prior day's low, pulls back. The 0.618 pocket lines up with that prior-day low and the rising 21-EMA. A hammer prints on the 5-min. Enter on its close, stop $0.15–0.25 below the low, target the day's high. CL moves fast — MCL (1/10 the size) is the sane place to learn.

S&P — SHORT ES/MES

ES pushes to a new all-time high but prints a shooting star with RSI divergence, then loses VWAP. Short the VWAP reclaim failure, stop above the ATH wick, target the prior support. Learn on MES first — it's 1/10 of ES and the tuition is 1/10 the price.

Order of operations Levels first → trend second → Fibonacci third → candle last → size the trade before you click. The candle is the trigger, never the reason.

07  /  The platforms

TopStep vs. Pocket Option — not the same game

TopStep · real futures

You trade CME futures (CL, ES, and micros) in an evaluation. Legitimate, regulated products. You keep a share of profits if you pass and stay within the loss rules. Everything in this guide applies directly. The hard part is the discipline the rules force — which is the point.

Pocket Option · binary options

A fixed win/lose bet on a price tick, with a payout structured so the house wins over time. Banned for US, UK, and EU retail traders, and heavily associated with scams. Reading candles well cannot overcome negative-expectancy math. This is the one place I'd tell you not to apply these skills.

Straight talk If money is tight, the honest move is: practice on a free simulator / paper account for weeks until your journal shows a real edge, learn on micros (MCL/MES) where a mistake costs a few dollars, and skip binary-options platforms entirely. Nobody has ever traded their way out of a hole on Pocket Option.

This is education, not financial advice. Trading futures and options involves substantial risk of loss and is not suitable for everyone. You can lose more than you deposit. Nothing here predicts market movements or guarantees any result — no strategy, indicator, or tool can. Past behavior of a level or pattern does not guarantee future behavior. Only risk money you can afford to lose entirely, and consider speaking with a licensed financial professional about your situation.
